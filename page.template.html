The reply bot that cannot bluff

Summon it like you'd summon grok — @fallbot and a question, anywhere in a thread. The difference is a law, not a vibe: every sentence it answers with is verbatim from what it actually read, wearing the receipt that says which source said it — and when its reading doesn't cover your question, the answer is "I don't have that." A bot that cannot say "I don't know" is a bluffing machine with good grammar.

What grok is trying to do — and where it breaks

The ambition is real and worth taking seriously: an ambient intelligence summoned by mention in a live feed — reads the thread, answers in real time, fact-checks on demand, increasingly agentic. The break is documented: one study measured 94% incorrect citations; fact-checkers catalogue answers wrong "not by a whisker, but by miles" — delivered fluently, where nobody verifies. And beneath accuracy: it's rented, unauditable, tuned to a platform's engagement metric, and locked to one feed. (The weakness map is sididy's, from the estate's doctrine.)
